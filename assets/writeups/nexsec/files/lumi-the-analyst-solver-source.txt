 XIO I:1/0    (guard door closed)
 XIO I:1/1    (E-stop not pressed)
 XIC I:1/2    (LOCAL)
 XIO I:1/5    (no thermal overload)
The live rung still honours E-Stop, LOCAL and thermal overload — those inputs never appear in
an unsafe state while the motor runs. But the guard-door check is gone: the live rung no longer
examines I:1/0 at all. Instead the rung's guard permissive is the maintenance keyswitch:
 XIC I:1/3    (maintenance key inserted) <-- replaces XIO I:1/0
An XIC on I:1/3 is exactly what a technician would write to turn the "maintenance override" into a
permanent permissive: the rung passes whenever the key is turned, regardless of the guard
door. That matches the operator story ("it runs with the door open") and the annunciator
evidence (BypassActive_Lamp lit).
So the single differing contact is:
                                 Instruction                      Address

 Baseline                        XIO                              I:1/0 (GuardDoor_Open)

 Live                            XIC                              I:1/3 (MaintKey_Inserted)
The programme is therefore not a simple bit inversion — it is a permissive substitution: the
guard-door check was replaced by the maintenance-key check.

   6.​ Building the Flag

 esident_checksum>}
with : and / in addresses replaced by _ (so I:1/0 → I_1_0).
